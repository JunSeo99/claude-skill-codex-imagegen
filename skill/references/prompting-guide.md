# Craft notes — read only the relevant section

## Text and posters

Quote the final copy, including punctuation and line breaks. Define hierarchy and
placement; keep safe margins for the target crop. Specify language, legibility,
and no additional copy. For Hangul inspect complete syllables, spelling, and spacing.
Never invent product claims, prices, or a slogan just to fill the design.
For a web hero, prefer real page typography over baked-in paragraphs. If generated
lettering remains wrong after one correction, disclose it and offer a deterministic
HTML/SVG text overlay. Do not silently remove requested text.

## Product photography

Describe what the surface should look like: brushed metal, translucent glass,
matte ceramic, woven fabric. Name the light source and direction, viewing angle,
contact shadows, and scale. Use a product reference for packaging or exact geometry.
Inspect labels, openings, handles, reflections, and contact with the ground.

## People

Choose documentary, studio, or editorial intent; specify crop, action, gaze, and
object contact when relevant. Natural skin texture and plausible light give the
reviewer concrete acceptance criteria. Check hands, teeth, limbs, and interactions.
Attach an identity anchor for preservation; avoid claiming exact identity fidelity.

## Hero art and small icons

Compose around the actual text area of the page. Keep the subject out of mobile
crop hazards. Test an icon at its intended display size; a detailed large image
can fail as a favicon. Request a strong silhouette and optical padding. A PNG
logo concept is not an editable vector or a completed brand identity.

## Diagrams and consistent sets

Have Claude establish correct labels and relationships before generation. Inspect
arrows and factual content separately from appearance. For a family of assets,
reuse the accepted visual anchor and a short style lock: palette, shape language,
light, texture, stroke weight, and padding. Keep individual briefs distinct.

## Review once, fix deliberately

Judge against the user's purpose rather than an invented numeric “quality score.”
Fix the highest-impact defect with one local instruction. Reattach the accepted
base; do not turn a color correction into a new composition. Keep successes.

The concise brief format is an editing convenience, not mandatory magic syntax.
